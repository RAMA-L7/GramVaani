## 2. Time-use and workload — quantitative findings

- `[VERIFIED]` `[STATE-MP]` AWWs spend a substantial share of the workday on **paper records**, not apps. Jain A, Walker DM, Avula R, et al. (2020, *BMC Health Serv Res* 20:1130) studied n=554 AWWs in 6 Madhya Pradesh districts: total work 359.4 min (~6 h)/day; **paper register work mean 51.1 min/day = 14% of workday** (median 47.5, max 180 min); government expected only 30 min/day, and **83% exceeded that expectation**. Corroborated by CRISP (2025, Andhra Pradesh, §below: 81.69 min/day paperwork/data entry = 17.02%) and by Kaur D, Thakur M, Singh A, Saini SK (2016, *Nursing and Midwifery Research Journal* 12(1):18-24), as summarised in the NIPCCD report: "records maintenance consumed ... three times more than the recommended time."
- `[VERIFIED]` `[STATE-MP]` `[STATE-AP]` The **same studies show app time is small in absolute terms**. Jain et al. 2020: "Updating Mobile App" mean **13.2 min/day = 4% of workday, median 0 min, max 180 min**. CRISP 2025 (AP): "Filling details on YSR Sampoorna Poshana App" **3.04% of day (~14.6 min/8-h day)** and "any other App" **1.99% (~9.6 min)** — i.e. roughly 24 min/day combined, versus ~58 min/day on paper registers. Both sources listed above.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-AP]` CRISP, Centre for Research in Schemes and Policies (Hyderabad, January 2025), *Time-Use Study of Anganwadi Workers in Andhra Pradesh: Insights for Strengthening Early Childhood Education*, submitted to the Director, WCD Department, Government of AP. Study team: Neha Ashar, Vijaya Raghavan, Sandesh Reddy, Manisha Koppala. Purposive sample of 30 AWCs across 17 districts; 28 observed over 8 days (9 AM–4 PM); **21 AWWs' data analysed** after cleaning (15 districts; 12 co-located, 9 standalone). Within-AWC activity shares of an 8-h day: preschool/ECE **39.34% (~189 min)**, supplementary nutrition 14.68% (~70 min), **paperwork/data entry 17.02% (~81.69 min; range 1.16%–49.4%)**, personal work 12.98% (~62 min), AWC maintenance 4.31% (~20 min). Activities outside the AWC (home visits, travel, vaccination drives) were **not systematically captured**.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Government of India, Ministry of WCD, Lok Sabha Unstarred Question No. 1121, answered 24 July 2026 by Minister of State Savithri Thakur: digital reporting on Poshan Tracker "rather [than] manual data entry in 11 physical registers" has **"substantially [reduced] workload and operational challenges of AWWs"**; offline data entry with sync within 5 days, smartphones provided to frontline functionaries, internet charges funded, 17-language helpline (1515) and daily 3–4 PM virtual classroom. This is an **official assertion with no time measurements attached** — labelled `[UNVERIFIED]` for the burden conclusion (see §6).
- `[UNVERIFIED]` `[NATIONAL]` NIPCCD Regional Centre Lucknow, *Time Management and Additional Work Deployment of Anganwadi Workers* (report date not printed in the PDF; latest cited literature 2018, so c. 2018–19), n=112 AWWs across 14 ICDS projects in 7 districts of UP, Bihar, Jharkhand and Himachal Pradesh: additional duties commonly took **5–10 h** (e.g. pulse polio: 46.8% UP, 59.37% Bihar, 93.75% Jharkhand, 93.75% HP). These are non-app "additional work deployment" hours, not app time; report date and authorship need verification before citation.
- `[UNKNOWN]` `[NATIONAL]` `[STATE-AP]` No reliable published measurement of "hours per day/week specifically in Poshan Tracker" or "specifically in Bala Sanjeevini" was located. No credible before/after digitisation **time-use** dataset (hours on reporting before vs after app rollout) was located. The closest before/after evidence measures service delivery, not worker time (Patil et al. 2022, §4).

## 3. Documentation and reporting burden — qualitative findings

- `[VERIFIED]` `[STATE-MP]` `[NATIONAL]` **Dual reporting / paper failsafe persists after digitisation.** Jain et al. 2020: "AWWs spend a big share of their work time on filling out their paper registers even when a smartphone-based application is available, and they had been given the option to not use the paper registers"; app-equipped AWWs were still required to keep paper registers "as a failsafe." Thakur A, Bhageerathy R, Mithra P, Sekaran VC, Kumar S (2025, *Healthcare (Basel)* 13(17):2223) record an AWW in an aspirational district: "We have to maintain both app entries and WhatsApp reporting—double work." (Second independent source: Scott et al. 2022 below, on handwritten data copied multiple times.)
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-KARNATAKA]` Gnanaselvam NA, Johnson AR, Shetty A (2025, *J Family Med Prim Care* 14(3):1029-1035), before/after training study with 381 AWWs in Anekal Taluk, Bengaluru Urban District: an AWW stated **"Poshan tracker and the need to do data entry has complicated our lives"**; authors observed that "appropriate tools such as right devices and Internet connect were an issue among the AWWs."
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-AP]` CRISP 2025 qualitative interviews (n not separately reported; conducted after the 8-day observation): "the time spent on documentation and app-based reporting has increased significantly"; AWWs perceived administrative work as **30 min–1 h daily on average, "though some days required up to 4–6 hours"**; they wanted more ECE time but felt quality time was taken by documentation, supplementary nutrition and THR distribution.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-MP]` `[STATE-RAJASTHAN]` Scott K, Ummer O, Chamberlain S, et al. (2022, *BMJ Open* 12:e051193), 59 interviews + 12 FGDs + 9 observations: FLHWs (ANMs/ASHAs) "found data collection ... too time intensive," felt "their work had shifted from healthcare provision to finding documents and filling in forms"; data were "handwritten and copied multiple times"; supervision "focused on completeness rather than accuracy."
- `[UNVERIFIED]` `[STATE-AP]` Our participant's origin claim — "workers report spending considerable time in government apps including Poshan Tracker and Bala Sanjeevini" — is a single unverified participant report. It is **not** supported as stated by the published time-use data (§2, §6).

## 4. Digital app usability, connectivity and language

- `[VERIFIED]` `[NATIONAL]` `[STATE-AP-relevant]` **Connectivity/server and device problems undermine official apps.** Thakur et al. 2025: an AWW says "When the server is working fine, the process is quicker. [But] ... the server is also a problem"; WhatsApp is used when "government apps (e.g., ANMOL, POSHAN Tracker) were inoperable due to network or server problems"; rural workers reported poor-quality phones, patchy networks and out-of-pocket data. Independent corroboration: Gnanaselvam et al. 2025 (devices and internet connectivity a problem) and Dalal et al. / Cureus Assam 2026 (below).
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Jain S, Ramakrishna S, Madan T, Gagan (2026, *Int J Med Inform* 222:106705, doi:10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2026.106705): cross-sectional survey of **1,055 stakeholders incl. 350 AWWs** for a child hearing-screening mHealth system; smartphone use 96.7%, daily use 76.5%, only 19.6% had earphones; willingness to use 68.3%, trust in explained AI outputs 78.9%; **time constraints 77.4% and anticipated increased workload 80.9%** were prominent barriers; preferred 6–10 min screening, 1–3 steps, multimodal guidance and offline functionality. Caveat: these percentages are pooled across teachers, AWWs and parents, not AWW-only; AWWs had significantly lower odds of willingness than teachers (OR 2.10, 95% CI 1.40–3.10).
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Patil SR, Nimmagadda S, Gopalakrishnan L, et al. (2022, *BMJ Glob Health* 6(Suppl 5):e007298): quasi-experimental evaluation of ICDS-CAS in MP and Bihar; ~98% of intervention AWWs trained, 100% (MP)/95% (Bihar) owned smartphones, ~70% reported always using app content during home visits. Adequate home visits rose by **+8 pp in both states** (MP comparison mean 42%, Bihar 24%); ≥50% correct counselling messages rose **+8–12 pp**; no effect on infant/young-child feeding practices. This is the best before/after digitisation outcome evidence, but it measures **service delivery, not worker time**.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Jaacks LM, Awasthi A, Kalra A (2024, *Lancet Reg Health Southeast Asia* 25:100381): Poshan Tracker is described as the world's largest mobile phone-based nutrition surveillance system; AWC open ≥15 days/month rose 35% (Jul 2022) → 89% (Sep 2023), and children measured 40.1 m (45%) → 83.55 m (94%). Authors caution increases "may be partially attributable to improved reporting" rather than service change, and report Poshan Tracker underestimating undernutrition vs NFHS-5 (underweight −13.72 pp, wasting −12.16 pp, stunting −1.86 pp); spring scales vs SECA scales and worker reporting bias are named explanations. Directly relevant to data-quality interpretation, not workload quantification.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Shukla R, Kapur A (2022, *Front Public Health* 10:894390): ICDS-CAS digitised and automated **10 of 11 paper registers**; "Studies have found that AWWs spend substantial amounts of time on administrative tasks. ICDS-CAS was thus envisaged to reduce this burden"; >600,000 workers trained and INR 640 crore (~USD 9.1 m) spent per an RTI request; CAS replaced by Poshan Tracker in September 2020.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Singh NS, Scott K, George A, et al. (2021, *BMJ Glob Health* 6(Suppl 5):e005041): CAS replaced 10/11 paper registers; respondents reported lower-level managers and FLHWs "struggled with using smartphones"; stakeholders debated rapid switch from paper vs a period of dual systems; one government informant said data were generated "for central decision making ... not for the FLHWs."
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-ASSAM]` Cureus (2026), *Frontline Health Workers' Perceptions of Digital Health Platforms: Qualitative Insights From the North Guwahati Block in Assam* (PMC13440629; 30 FLWs): "I spend more time typing than attending to patients" (ANM); "We enter data in portals and still maintain paper registers" (ANM); one-time brief trainings insufficient; unreliable internet most frequent barrier; participants recommended **voice-based data entry in regional languages**. Comparable cadre (ANM/ASHA/MPW), not AWWs.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` `[NATIONAL]` `[STATE-AP]` Government's July 2026 claims of offline entry, 5-day sync, smartphone provision, funded connectivity, 17-language helpline and daily virtual classrooms (Lok Sabha Q1121) are official statements of provision. No independent study in this search verified their field-level reach or effect on AWW time.
- `[UNKNOWN]` `[STATE-AP]` No published usability study specific to Bala Sanjeevini or YSR Sampoorna Poshana App was found. The AP official site (balasanjeevani.ap.gov.in) confirms the AP WCD Department's ICDS platform covering 55,607 AWCs across 26 districts, but gives no time-use or usability data; CRISP 2025 measured the YSR Sampoorna Poshana App as a separate app label. Do not equate the two without field confirmation.

## 5. Voice/assistive interventions and measured outcomes

- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Bashingwa JJH, LeFevre A, Chauhan V, et al. (2021, *BMJ Glob Health* 6(Suppl 5):e005299): **Mobile Academy**, an interactive voice response (IVR) refresher course for ASHAs across 13 states; 158,596 ASHAs initiated the national version and 111,994 a state version (=41% of registered ASHAs); **81% of national-version initiates completed, >99% of those passed**; ASHAs spent **~5 h over 10 calls** to complete, 15% spent 240–260 min. Some ASHAs could not navigate IVR menus but still benefited by listening. Measured outcomes are reach/completion, not health or workload outcomes.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-RAJASTHAN]` Scott K, Ummer O, Chamberlain S, et al. (2022, *BMJ Open* 12:e050363): qualitative study (25 IDIs, 5 FGDs) of Mobile Academy; ASHAs praised the friendly recorded voice and found Hindi speech easy to understand despite speaking Marwadi; audio let them learn at home and avoid travel to training centres. Authors explicitly did **not** evaluate knowledge or practice effects.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Sivasubramanian S, Raval A (2025, *Health Affairs Scholar* 3(5):qxaf097): account of AI-augmented public health in India — "we underestimated the effort required for health officers to enter data. This made adoption difficult. To fix this, we are designing a **voice-based interface** that makes data collection easier and faster"; also recommends multilingual voice-based extraction of codified data from speech. This is a design response to the problem; **no measured outcome of the voice interface is reported**.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[STATE-MP]` Tomar N, Srikrishnan S, Lesh N, DeRenzi B (2023, *Global Health: Science and Practice* 11(4), doi:10.9745/GHSP-D-22-00148): **Poshan Didi** text chatbot piloted in Katni district, MP (Phase 1 n=10 mothers; Phase 2 n=100); most messages answered in <5 h; 64 escalations to a nurse; users valued fast responses. Implementation finding: typing skill did not equal chatbot ability — keyboard and **local-language typing training** had to be added. Beneficiary-facing, text-based, not AWW reporting; authors state efficacy/cost-effectiveness still untested.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` `[NATIONAL]` Schwartz A, Balcarras M, Tiwari A, et al. (2026, *JMIR* 28(1):e86326): WhatsApp PCV chatbot for ANMs across 4 states; rated useful, saved time finding information; barriers included **difficulty typing regional languages**, connectivity gaps, and concern it might add workload; respondents explicitly recommended a **voice-enabled** bot and predictive text. Comparable frontline cadre; qualitative acceptability, not measured time savings.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` `[NATIONAL]` Media/vendor reports of **ASHABot** (Microsoft Research/Khushi Baby) and **HealthVaani** (Wadhwani AI) describe voice/AI tools for ASHA/AWW cadres, but no peer-reviewed evaluation with measured outcomes was located in PubMed/Europe PMC. Treat as [UNVERIFIED] until a protocol/results paper is found.
- `[UNKNOWN]` No peer-reviewed trial of **voice-driven data entry/reporting by AWWs** measuring time saved, error rates, or acceptance was found. This is a genuine evidence gap and a plausible niche for GramVaani — but it is an inference about a gap, not evidence of benefit.

## 6. PUBLISHED EVIDENCE vs our UNVERIFIED project claim

- `[UNVERIFIED]` `[STATE-AP]` **Our origin claim as recorded in `research/RESEARCH_FINDINGS.md`**: "workers report spending considerable time in government apps including Poshan Tracker and Bala Sanjeevini" — currently `[hypothesis]`, zero verified findings. It is one participant's report and remains `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- `[VERIFIED]` **What published evidence does support:** AWWs devote a substantial slice of the workday to **documentation/administration** — principally **paper registers** — 51.1 min/day (14%) in MP (Jain et al. 2020) and 81.69 min/day (17.02%) in AP (CRISP 2025). It also supports duplicate reporting (Jain et al. 2020; Thakur et al. 2025), connectivity/server/device problems (Thakur et al. 2025; Gnanaselvam et al. 2025), and perceived increases in documentation/app reporting (CRISP 2025).
- `[VERIFIED]` **What published evidence does not support as stated:** that AWWs spend "considerable time **in apps**." The two time-use studies that actually measured app time found **13.2 min/day (4%, median 0)** for app updating in MP (2020) and approximately **15 min/day for the state app plus 10 min/day for "any other App"** in AP (2025) — about 5% of the 8-h day. "Considerable time" is a judgement, but no published measurement puts app time in the range of hours/day.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` **Official counter-position:** GOI's answer of 24 July 2026 asserts Poshan Tracker has "substantially reduced" AWW workload by replacing 11 physical registers. It provides no comparative time data, so it cannot settle the question either way; it does, however, make an unqualified "apps take considerable time" framing contestable.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` **Verdict (inference about our project claim):** the project's origin claim should **not** be upgraded from `[hypothesis]`. A defensible evidence-based reformulation would be: *"Published studies document considerable time on documentation and registers, with smaller but burdensome app-based entry, frequent duplicate entry and connectivity problems; the specific claim that AWWs spend considerable time inside Poshan Tracker/Bala Sanjeevini is unverified and is contradicted by the only two time-use measurements available."* Keep the original claim labelled `[UNVERIFIED]` in all project records.

## 7. Methodological cautions and gaps

- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` **Small, non-random samples:** CRISP 2025 analysed only 21 AWWs (30 planned, 28 observed, 21 after cleaning) over 8 days, inside the AWC 9 AM–4 PM; home visits and other off-site work "not systematically captured"; 3 tribal AWCs were too few for inference. Jain et al. 2020 is larger (n=554, 6 MP districts) but still one state.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` **Self-report/recall bias:** Jain et al. 2020 used stylised questions about time on each activity on the day of data collection (pilot involved shadowing 36 AWWs to build the activity list); CRISP's qualitative interviews were self-reported perceptions of "30 min–1 h ... up to 4–6 h" administrative time. Neither is a full time-and-motion diary of app use.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` **Different app regimes:** Jain 2020 was conducted under ICDS-CAS (before Poshan Tracker replaced CAS in Sep 2020); CRISP 2025 observed YSR Sampoorna Poshana App; Thakur 2025 and the 2026 Lok Sabha answer concern Poshan Tracker. Findings are not interchangeable across systems or years.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` **Regional variation:** evidence comes from MP, Bihar, AP, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Assam and Delhi-area studies; only CRISP 2025 is AP-specific. Capacities, registers and app ecosystems differ across states; national figures should not be assumed for AP.
- `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` **Source-quality caveats:** NIPCCD report date/authorship unclear; some relevant items are preprints (Thakur et al. 2025 preprint version) or single-block qualitative studies (Cureus Assam 2026); a 2026 Cureus paper on take-home ration barriers in MP is **retracted** and was excluded. Chatbot/IVR studies measure acceptability, reach or knowledge, not worker time savings.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` **Government vs independent framing:** official statements describe support and reduced burden; independent qualitative studies describe duplication and "invisible overtime." Both may be partly true; the truth is likely task- and context-specific.
- `[UNKNOWN]` **No before/after worker-time dataset** for app introduction, no AWW voice-reporting trial with measured outcomes, and no CAG audit section quantifying AWW documentation hours were found.
