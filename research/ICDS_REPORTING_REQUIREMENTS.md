# ICDS Reporting Requirements and Responsibilities
_Agent 3 | Access date: 2026-11-16 | Status: research notes, no application changes_

> **Scope guard for the GramVaani study:** This file records only what published sources state. GramVaani is a Telugu-first voice-assisted *research prototype* for Anganwadi workers (AWWs) in Andhra Pradesh. It has **no government integration, no submissions, no beneficiary data, and makes no official-compatibility claim**. Nothing below should be read as a legal obligation of the prototype, as a required report field, or as permission to collect beneficiary information. No private accounts or beneficiary records were accessed during this research.

## 1. Scope and method

- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **What was searched:** official Ministry of Women and Child Development (MWCD/MoWCD) portals (`wcd.gov.in`, `spniwcd.wcd.gov.in`), Press Information Bureau (PIB), the scheme guideline PDF hosted by the Delhi WCD Department, an ICDS District Manual hosted by the WHO platform, a World Bank learning note, a state government AWW roles document (Tripura), a civil-society capacity-building report (Sattva), and two Andhra Pradesh government portals (`wdcw.ap.gov.in`, `wcdsc.ap.gov.in`, `balasanjeevani.ap.gov.in`). Sources: [2], [3], [4], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Labelling rule applied:** `[VERIFIED]` = the same factual point was found in 2+ independent credible sources (both named in the finding); `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` = one credible official/academic source only; `[UNVERIFIED]` = inference, secondary retelling, or participant-type statement; `[UNKNOWN]` = searched, not established. `[NATIONAL]` = central/GoI-level; `[STATE-AP]` = Andhra Pradesh-specific.
- [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [NATIONAL] **Why older documents are still cited:** the current scheme guidelines (2021) explicitly state that the Streamlined Guidelines dated 13.01.2021 are "an integral part" of them and repeatedly defer detailed process/reporting questions to those earlier guidelines [2]. The 2017 ICDS District Manual [7] is therefore relevant background, **but it predates Poshan 2.0 and the Poshan Tracker and may no longer describe live practice**. It is cited for what it documents, with an explicit age warning, not as a claim about 2026 operations.
- [UNKNOWN] [NATIONAL] **An "AWW diary" as a named, nationally prescribed reporting instrument was not established.** Searches returned only commercial diary products and student/field-observation write-ups on document-sharing sites, not an official MWCD or AP government document defining an "Anganwadi Worker diary" [search, 2026-11-16]. The officially named documents that *were* found are listed in §3. Do not assume a diary format exists or invent its fields.
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Terminology caution:** official English documents use "Lady Supervisor"/"Supervisor", "Child Development Project Officer (CDPO)", and "District Programme Officer (DPO)". "Mukhyasevika" is the Hindi-language equivalent of Supervisor and appears in commercial/secondary material, not in the primary English sources used here [2], [7].

## 2. Policy and scheme landscape (MWCD, Saksham, POSHAN 2.0)

- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Nodal ministry and legal basis.** The Anganwadi Services/ICDS programme is anchored by the Ministry of Women and Child Development, Government of India [3], [7]. The Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 scheme guidelines state they are issued under Sections 4(a), 5(1)(a), 6 and 7 of the **National Food Security Act, 2013**, and "shall come into force with immediate effect" [2]. This is the closest thing in the reviewed sources to a statutory basis for services; it is **not** a documented legal obligation for any fourth-party software prototype. Sources: [2], [3], [7].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Current scheme structure.** Anganwadi Services (earlier ICDS, launched 1975) has been revised and subsumed under **Mission Saksham Anganwadi & Poshan 2.0 (Mission Poshan 2.0)** [3], [4], [11]. The mission brings together Anganwadi Services, Poshan Abhiyaan and the Scheme for Adolescent Girls (SAG) [3], [12], [13]. It is a **Centrally Sponsored Mission**, with implementation responsibility lying with States/UTs [2], [12], [13]. Sources: [2], [3], [12], [13].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Four primary verticals** (identical lists in the official guidelines and the SPNIWCD FAQ): (i) Nutrition Support through Supplementary Nutrition Programme (SNP) for 6 months–6 years, pregnant women and lactating mothers, and adolescent girls 14–18 in Aspirational Districts/NER; (ii) Early Childhood Care and Education (3–6 years) and early stimulation (0–3 years); (iii) Anganwadi Infrastructure including modern, upgraded Saksham Anganwadi; (iv) Poshan Abhiyaan [2], [3]. Sources: [2], [3], [9].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Approval period:** approved for the 15th Finance Commission period **2021-22 to 2025-26** [2], [9]. Note that this period ends in 2026; sources retrieved on 2026-11-16 still describe the mission in the present tense [12], [13], but **whether a successor scheme/guideline has since been notified was [UNKNOWN] at access time**.
- [UNVERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Launch date — exact day not established.** Official sources consistently say the mission was "launched" and give the **year 2021** and the Budget 2021-22 consolidation, but the reviewed official pages do not state an exact launch calendar date [2], [9], [13]. Secondary/paraphrase working papers likewise give only "2021" (not tabled as a primary source here). **Record: launched/announced in 2021; exact date [UNKNOWN].** The related "guidelines issued" PIB item shows a posting date of 02 AUG 2022 while the guideline PDF itself is titled "Final guidelines Saksham Anganwadi Poshan 2.0 July 29" — likely a re-post; treat the date as conflicting [1], [2].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Objectives stated in official sources:** contribute to human capital development; address malnutrition; promote nutrition awareness and good eating habits; address nutrition-related deficiencies; focus on maternal nutrition, IYCF norms, SAM/MAM treatment protocols and AYUSH wellness [2], [9], [11]. Sources: [2], [3], [9].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Six package services at the AWC:** supplementary nutrition, pre-school non-formal education, nutrition & health education, immunisation, health check-up, referral services. Immunisation, health check-up and referral are delivered through NHM/public health infrastructure (ASHA/ANM/MO), not by AWWs alone [2], [3], [11-AP]. Sources: [2], [3], [11-AP].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **What changed from earlier ICDS (as documented):** reorganisation of existing components into four verticals; new focus on diet diversity/quality rather than calorie sufficiency alone; new "Saksham Anganwadi" upgrading of 2 lakh AWCs @ 40,000/year with LED screens, RO/water purifiers, smart learning/audio-visual aids, Poshan Vatikas, Wi-Fi via BharatNet where available; **Poshan Tracker as a new centralised ICT data system** linked to the RCH Portal (Anmol); SAG revised from out-of-school girls 11–14 to girls 14–18 in Aspirational Districts/NER; nutrition norms noted as under revision [2], [3], [9]. Sources: [2], [3], [9].
- [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [NATIONAL] **Nutrition norms superseded:** the 2021 guidelines' tables note "Nutritional Norms are under revision and will be notified shortly" [2]. A 2026 PIB reply states the Schedule-II NFSA 2013 norms were **revised in January 2023** to be more comprehensive (quantity + quality, diet diversity, protein, healthy fats, micronutrients) [13]. Do not rely on the 2021 calorie/protein/cost tables as current.

## 3. National-level reporting formats, registers and cadence

- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Registers/records attributed to the AWW.** The ICDS District Manual (2017) lists: Family Register; Supplementary Food Stock Register; Supplementary Food Distribution Register; Pre-School Education Register; Pregnancy and Delivery Register; Immunisation and Village Health and Nutrition Day (VHND) register; Vitamin A biannual rounds register; **Home Visits Planner**; Referrals; Summaries (**monthly and annual**); Weight Records of Children [7]. Independently, a World Bank (2024) learning note states AWWs were mandated to maintain ICDS data manually in **a set of 11 paper registers**, covering beneficiary registration, growth monitoring and promotion, supplementary nutrition distribution, home visits, pre-school education, and immunisation [8]. A Sattva Knowledge Institute (2023) report likewise says AWWs/helpers maintain records in 11 registers across the six functions [10]. Sources: [7], [8], [10]. **The two lists are not identical — 11 is a frequently repeated figure, but no single authoritative national source in this review enumerates the definitive "11".** Do not reconstruct a form from these lists.
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Growth monitoring and referral documentation.** AWW duties in the 2021 guidelines: weigh each child every month, **record the weight graphically on the growth card**, use a **referral card** to refer mothers/children to sub-centres/PHC, maintain child cards for children below 6 years and produce them to visiting medical/para-medical personnel [2]. The Tripura state government's AWW roles document repeats the same duties nearly verbatim [11]. Sources: [2], [11]. Growth measurement is stated as every month for both height and weight for children 6 months–6 years, with a missed month to be compulsorily measured the following month [2].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Food/supply registers.** "Proper receipts and registers shall be maintained by the AWW for food grains supplied (Wheat, Fortified Rice, Millets, Coarse Grains, Dal and other edibles etc.) to the AWC"; oversight/audits by VHSNC, Lady Supervisor and CDPO **on a monthly basis**; food-grain allocation and utilisation to be monitored under Poshan Tracker "as and when the module ... is developed by NeGD" [2]. A separate official WIFS-format example (Format 7A) shows a **monthly AWC reporting form with an IFA/Albendazole supply section (opening balance, stock received, utilised, closing balance) and signature lines for Anganwadi Worker and ICDS Supervisor**; it is a *health-sector WIFS* format, not a general ICDS format [5]. Sources: [2], [5].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Reporting cadence — older ICDS chain (documented, may be superseded).** The 2017 ICDS District Manual gives: AWW → Supervisor, Anganwadi Level Report **by the 2nd working day of the following month**; Supervisor → CDPO, consolidated cluster report **by the 5th**; CDPO → DPO/SMU, consolidated block report **by the 10th**; SMU → MWCD, consolidated state report **by the 16th** [7]. The same manual says revised MIS/RRS introduced new formats of registers and Monthly Progress Reports (MPR) and Annual Status Reports (ASR) at AWW and CDPO level, with revamped AW-MPR replacing AW-MPR and AW-ASR [7]. **This is a 2017 document; Poshan Tracker is now the stated primary reporting channel. Treat the dates as historical, not verified live requirements.** [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] for the specific dates (single source [7]).
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Current reporting duty wording.** The 2021 guidelines require the AWW "to feed relevant beneficiary data in Poshan Tracker app on regular basis as instructed from time to time and **generate/submit reports/ returns as specified**", and "to maintain files and records as prescribed" [2]. CDPO duties include "**ensure collation of monthly progress reports for the District and share with the State**" and conducting joint field visits and monthly joint review meetings with the Medical Officer, especially for SAM children [2]. Sources: [2], [3].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Poshan Panchayat reporting (not AWW personal reporting).** Poshan Panchayats monitor nutritional status month-to-month, SAM/MAM status **every fortnight**, and CBEs; their **quarterly reports** are made available to the CDPO and DPO and may be presented to the District Nutrition Committee chaired by the DM/Collector [2]. DPOs must set up regular review mechanisms and reporting on Poshan Panchayats to the District Magistrate [2]. Sources: [2], [3].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Daily data points actually named in current official descriptions.** A 2026 PIB parliamentary reply says Poshan Tracker enables near-real-time collection of: **opening of AWCs, daily attendance of children, ECCE activities, growth monitoring, distribution of Hot Cooked Meal and Take-Home Ration**; a **Facial Recognition System (FRS)** has been introduced for THR distribution [13]. SPNIWCD describes Poshan Tracker as digitising/automating physical registers used by AWWs [4]. Sources: [4], [13]. **This is a description of a government system's capabilities; it is not a specification for GramVaani and must not be treated as an integration target.**
- [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [NATIONAL] **Community Based Events (CBEs):** each AWC organises CBEs **twice per month**, supported at ₹250 each; a 2026 PIB reply states "all Anganwadi workers are required to conduct two Community Based Events every month" [2], [13].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Supervision chain documented:** Supervisor (Lady Supervisor) typically heads ~20 AWCs (2017 manual) / 1 per 25 main AWCs (2021 cost norms) and reports to the CDPO; the 2017 manual says the Supervisor **visits every Anganwadi once a month** and facilitates maintenance of registers in prescribed formats, compiles monitoring reports of AWWs and shares feedback [2], [7]. CDPO is block-level implementation head; District Magistrate/Collector is district nodal point for monitoring nutritional status and quality standards [2], [7]. Sources: [2], [7].
- [UNKNOWN] [NATIONAL] **No source reviewed supplied a complete, current, nationally prescribed AWC monthly report template with all its fields.** Format 7A [5] is a WIFS-specific example; the ICDS MPR/ASR formats referred to in [7] were not retrievable as current official templates. Do not infer or reconstruct fields.

## 4. AWW and supervisor responsibilities

- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **AWW responsibilities directly about documentation/reporting in the 2021 guidelines** (verbatim substance): learn to use the smartphone provided to the AWC; feed beneficiary data into Poshan Tracker regularly and generate/submit reports and returns as specified; monthly weighing + graphical growth-card recording + referral card + child cards; carry out a quick survey of all families at least once a year; maintain files and records as prescribed; assist ANM with IFA/Vitamin A **without maintaining a stock register** (explicitly to reduce administrative work); share information collected under ICDS with the ANM (ANM must not rely solely on those records); bring developments in the village to the notice of Supervisors/CDPO; guide ASHA in delivery of health care services and **maintenance of records** under Poshan 2.0; maintain prescribed records for SAG/NPAG-type activities; maintain COVID-19 protocols; inform ANM in emergencies (diarrhoea, cholera, etc.) [2], [11]. Sources: [2], [11] (near-identical duty lists in central and state documents).
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **AWW status:** AWWs and Anganwadi Helpers are described as **"honorary workers"** from the local community rendering part-time service; they receive monthly honoraria [2], [10]. Minimum qualification 12th standard, age 18–35 for new engagements; retirement age decided by the State/UT, but not beyond 65 [2]. Sources: [2], [10].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Helper duties** (2021 guidelines): cook/serve food, clean the AWC premises daily and fetch water, ensure cleanliness of young children, bring children from the village, help the AWW, and **in the AWW's absence carry out her functions** [2], [11]. Sources: [2], [11].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Supervisor duties (documented):** guide AWWs in surveys/beneficiary listing; visit every AWC once a month; attend at least 2 monthly Mother–Child Days; attend at least two ECCE days every month; facilitate maintenance of registers in prescribed formats; check entries of births/deaths in the survey register and immunisation register; check storage/preparation/distribution of food and stocks and **report shortages to the CDPO**; compile monitoring reports of AWWs and share feedback; ensure preparation of required reports at all levels; organise monthly meetings of AWWs in her circle with LHVs/ANMs [7]. Sources: [7] plus [2] for the CDPO/DM framework.
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **CDPO/DPO/DM duties (documented):** CDPO — overall administration/coordination, periodic monitoring incl. surprise spot-checks and sample collection for quality testing, participation in VHSND/CBEs, joint field visits and monthly joint reviews with MO (especially SAM), collation of monthly progress reports for the District shared with the State, home visits during AWC visits [2]. DM/Collector — district nodal point for monitoring nutritional status and quality standards, chairs District Nutrition Committee [2], [7]. Sources: [2], [7].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Workload/effort context (not an official duty statement):** the World Bank note records that a key objective of introducing digital tools was to spare AWWs from maintaining 11 paper registers, but states this has **not yet been achieved** because of fear of data loss, and notes "dual recording of data on the ground" [8]. Sattva (2023) reports ~21 responsibilities, 11 registers, and a frontline-worker quote "Don't make us fill-up more registers" [10]. These are **published research/advocacy findings, not legal requirements**. Sources: [8], [10].

## 5. ICDS-CAS and digitisation policy (status / caution)

- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **ICDS-CAS history and replacement.** The World Bank learning note (2024) documents that ICDS Common Application Software (ICDS-CAS) was introduced in phases from 2018 as a mobile and web-based application for service delivery, supervision, monitoring and reporting; by March 2020 around **606,278 AWCs in 24 states** used it; MWCD revamped it in 2021 into **POSHAN Tracker**, fully developed/managed/owned by NeGD (MeiTY) [8]. The note explicitly describes "when ICDS-CAS was replaced by POSHAN Tracker" and an "application switch from ICDS-CAS to POSHAN Tracker" [8]. **ICDS-CAS should be treated as a superseded/legacy system for this study** [8], [4].
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Poshan Tracker official status.** Rolled out **1 March 2021** via National e-Governance Division (NeGD), MyGov, as an ICT governance tool; real-time monitoring/tracking of all AWCs, AWWs and beneficiaries on defined indicators, Aadhaar-seeded, linked to RCH Portal (Anmol) [2], [4]. A 2026 PIB reply says "March 2021" and reports near-real-time indicators plus FRS for THR [13]. Sources: [2], [4], [13].
- [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [NATIONAL] **Contradictory launch date for Poshan Tracker:** a PIB document on Rashtriya Poshan Maah says "a robust ICT-enabled platform, 'POSHAN Tracker', was developed by the MoWCD and **launched on January 13, 2021**" [14]. This conflicts with "1 March 2021"/"March 2021" in [2], [4], [13]. Possible reading: January = software launch/demonstration, March = operational rollout — **this reconciliation is [UNVERIFIED] inference**. Do not state a single definitive date.
- [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Documented digitisation / effort-reduction intent.** (a) Guidelines: "IT systems are being leveraged to strengthen and bring about transparency in nutrition delivery support systems"; Poshan Tracker to provide data, feedback to programme managers and impact documentation, enabling timely interventions and continuous evaluation [2]. (b) Guidelines: scheme documents/information to be stored on servers **within India only**, with firewalls and NIC/other government-agency guidelines mandatory [2]. (c) SPNIWCD: the mobile app "digitizes and automates the physical registers used by Anganwadi Worker which helps improving their quality of work" [4]. (d) World Bank: objective to spare AWWs from 11 paper registers, not yet achieved; dashboard with key indicators publicly available; web dashboards at sector/block/district/state/national levels [8]. (e) 2017 manual: ICT-enabled Real Time Monitoring and an ICDS-RRS for real-time entry and monitoring of physical and financial progress [7]. Sources: [2], [4], [7], [8].
- [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [NATIONAL] **Data-protection framing:** the 2021 guidelines require India-located servers, firewalls and adherence to NIC/other government agency guidance [2]. This is a stated rule **for the government scheme's systems**; it is not a certification or compliance pathway for a research prototype.
- [UNKNOWN] [NATIONAL] **No source reviewed documents a formal "paperless reporting" mandate, a deadline for abolishing paper registers, or an API/integration route for third-party prototypes.** Do not claim one.

## 6. Andhra Pradesh-specific requirements — what is actually documented

- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **Nodal department and scale.** AP has a separate Department for Women Development & Child Welfare (WD&CW) / Department of Women, Children, Disabled & Senior Citizens; ICDS in AP since **2 October 1975**, universalised 2006 [11-AP], [12-AP]. The AP WD&CW portal reports **257 projects, 2,205 sectors, 55,776 Anganwadis, 8,459 tribal AWCs, 23.1 lakh total beneficiaries** (children 7m–3y 11.4 lakh; 3–6y 7.3 lakh; pregnant & lactating 4.4 lakh) [11-AP]. The WCDA&SC portal gives 257 ICDS projects (200 rural, 33 urban, 24 tribal), 48,770 main AWCs + 6,837 mini AWCs = 55,607 AWCs across 26 districts [12-AP]. Sources: [11-AP], [12-AP]. (The two portals agree on ~55.6k AWCs; 55,776 vs 55,607 is a minor internal inconsistency — flag, do not pick one silently.)
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP states its own objectives and six services** on the Saksham portal, mirroring national language, and names who delivers each: Supplementary Nutrition — AWWs and Helpers; Nutrition & Health Education — AWW, ANM and MO; Pre-school Education — AWWs; Immunisation — ANM/MO; Health check-ups — ANM/MO/AWW; Referrals — AWW/ANM/MO [11-AP]. Sources: [11-AP], [12-AP].
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP-specific service cadence documented:** THR distribution "twice a month, preferably on **1st Friday and 3rd Friday**"; supplementary nutrition served minimum 300 days/year (~25 days/month) [11-AP]. **This conflicts with the national guideline's "twice a month, preferably on 1st and 15th of each calendar month" [2] — see §8.**
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP-specific Nutrition and Health Days:** "conducted twice every month ... on 1st Friday with focus on Growth monitoring & THR; on 3rd Friday with focus on providing immunization and ANC services by ANM and counselling the mothers" [11-AP]. Single-source detail → `[PUBLISHED EVIDENCE]` for the two-Friday framing; the twice-monthly CBE requirement is national [2], [13].
- [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [STATE-AP] **AP-specific register mention:** "During health check-ups and growth monitoring, sick or malnourished children in need of prompt medical attention are provided referral services through ICDS. The AWW ... enlists all such cases in a **special register** and refers them to the medical officer" (in the context of detecting disabilities in young children) [11-AP]. This is the only AP-specific register explicitly named in the reviewed AP sources; its exact title is **not given**.
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP digital systems actually listed on the official portal:** Poshan Tracker (national system), **Bala Sanjeevani** (AP supplementary nutrition portal), **eSadhana MIS** (`mis.wdcw.ap.gov.in`), **Milk Supply Tracking System** (`apddcf.ap.gov.in/icdcsp`), **THR-FRS** (`wdcwfrs.ap.gov.in`), and a **"Supervisor's Supervision"** portal (`wdcwss.ap.gov.in/dashboard-1/`), plus an AWW/AWH recruitment system [11-AP], [12-AP]. Sources: [11-AP], [12-AP]. **Only their existence/names and linked home pages were observed; no login, internal dashboard, field list, or beneficiary data was accessed.** What each system records, and the reporting cadence into them, is **[UNKNOWN]**.
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP documents Poshan Tracker use:** "Real Time Monitoring of ICDS services through Poshan Tracker application — provide a 360-degree view of the activities of the AWC, service deliveries of AWWs and complete beneficiary management for pregnant women, lactating mothers and children" [12-AP], [11-AP]. Sources: [11-AP], [12-AP].
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP-specific budgeting (context only, not a reporting duty):** FY 2026-27 allocations shown as Saksham Anganwadi (ICDS) 60:40 ₹66,089.47 lakh; SNP 50:50 ₹78,843.04 lakh; SAG 50:50 ₹1,496.22 lakh; Poshan Abhiyaan 60:40 ₹16,182.58 lakh; total ₹151,304.49 lakh [11-AP]. Source: [11-AP].
- [VERIFIED] [STATE-AP] **AP-specific programme detail:** Balasanjeevani plain vs tribal (77 tribal scheduled/sub-plan mandals; 47,287 AWCs in plain areas); SAG for girls 14–18 in 3 aspirational districts (Alluri Sitarama Raju, Parvathipuram Manyam, YSR Kadapa); ECCE initiatives incl. 1,038 Hub AWCs, 41 Model Foundation Schools, "Poshan Bhi Padai Bhi" training and SALT/World Bank FLN work [11-AP], [12-AP]. Sources: [11-AP], [12-AP], [10].
- [UNKNOWN] [STATE-AP] **AP-specific AWW register list, AP-specific AWW diary format, AP-specific MPR/ASR template, AP-specific daily/weekly/monthly submission deadlines, and the AP-specific reporting chain for a prototype-style voice report were NOT established.** The AP portals describe services, coverage and digital systems; they do not publish a reporting-format manual. `[UNKNOWN]` after searching `wdcw.ap.gov.in`, `wcdsc.ap.gov.in`, `balasanjeevani.ap.gov.in` and the open web on 2026-11-16. **Do not fill this gap from national norms.**

## 8. Contradictions, outdated documents and cautions

1. [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **THR date conflict:** national guidelines say 1st and 15th [2]; AP portal says 1st Friday and 3rd Friday [11-AP]. State practice may legitimately differ, but the sources conflict on the face of it. Do not assert one as universal.
2. [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Poshan Tracker launch date conflict:** "1 March 2021" [2],[4] / "March 2021" [13] vs "13 January 2021" [14]. Reconciliation is `[UNVERIFIED]`.
3. [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Old vs new reporting channel:** the 2017 MPR/RRS chain and 11-register discipline [7],[8] coexist in the literature with the 2021+ Poshan Tracker mandate [2],[4],[13]. World Bank explicitly reports dual paper/digital recording persists [8]. **Do not assume paper registers have been abolished** and do not assume the 2017 deadlines are live.
4. [VERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **Superseded norms:** 2021 nutrition/cost tables were already flagged "under revision" [2]; PIB says norms revised January 2023 [13]. Guidelines' numeric norms are outdated.
5. [UNVERIFIED] [NATIONAL] **PIB guideline-release item date ambiguity:** PIB PRID 1847548 displays "02 AUG 2022" while the PDF is titled "July 29" and the scheme is a 2021 scheme [1],[2]. Likely a re-post; exact issue date not resolved.
6. [PUBLISHED EVIDENCE] [NATIONAL] **Scheme period boundary:** guidelines cover 2021-22 to 2025-26 [2]; no successor notification was located by 2026-11-16 → `[UNKNOWN]` for post-2025-26 status.
